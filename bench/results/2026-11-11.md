## Recovery, log growth and memory

Each timing is one fresh-process Open on a freshly prepared cached log. RSS deltas include index, allocator, scratch and runtime pages. They are a coarse resident-memory observation, not exact index heap allocation. Three timings/case make p95/p99 the largest observation.

| Mutations / live keys | Log bytes | Log/live-byte ratio | p50/p95/p99 ms | Median RSS delta KiB | Median open RSS KiB |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1,024 / 1,024 | 91,468 | 1.117 | 4.614/15.878/15.878 | 372 | 4,316 |
| 8,192 / 8,192 | 731,212 | 1.116 | 8.160/10.723/10.723 | 1,292 | 5,044 |
| 32,768 / 8,192 | 2,924,620 | 4.463 | 14.027/14.508/14.508 | 1,292 | 5,112 |

With the same 8,192 live keys, four times the mutations grew the log from 731,212 to 2,924,620 bytes and median Open time from 8.160 to 14.027 ms. The 1,024-key timings are noisy. Reported ru_maxrss was 18,284 KiB throughout, much higher than the 4–5 MiB open resident sets; treat this high-water statistic as launcher/runtime affected, not an index-size estimate.

## Offline compaction

Bytes are logical file lengths. The complete temporary file was observed before rename; peak logical source+temp excludes filesystem overhead. Physical Btrfs allocation/compression is not measured. Positive reclaimed bytes means shrinkage.

| Source | Before bytes | Output/temp bytes | Reclaimed bytes | Peak logical bytes | p50/p95/p99 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| clean grouped fill | 365,644 | 495,692 | -130,048 | 861,336 | 52.635/91.819/91.819 |
| four fills, delete 1/4 | 1,487,980 | 371,788 | +1,116,192 | 1,859,768 | 43.854/74.826/74.826 |

Clean grouped input grew by 130,048 bytes (35.6%) because compaction writes one frame per live key. The overwrite/delete workload reclaimed 1,116,192 bytes (75.0%). Temporary-space requirements and growth remain explicit operational costs.

## Shared-handle caller contention

Every caller performs durable singleton overwrites over the same 64 keys, with 1,024 total writes/run. Throughput is median mutations/s; pooled latencies include mutex wait.

| Callers | Mutations/s | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 140.0 | 6.870 | 20.810 | 35.741 |
| 2 | 124.1 | 4.689 | 15.563 | 39.517 |
| 4 | 134.9 | 4.582 | 15.224 | 26.330 |
| 8 | 123.7 | 4.616 | 19.185 | 702.995 |

There was no throughput scaling: 1 caller delivered about 140 writes/s and 8 delivered 124 writes/s. Eight callers had 702.995 ms p99 latency. Serialized I/O and uneven mutex admission are consistent with these observations; the benchmark does not isolate their individual contributions.

No external-engine comparison, cold-cache/device-write-amplification experiment, production-load model, or physical power-loss test was performed. Correctness gates were unchanged by these results. See [readiness](../../docs/verification.md).
